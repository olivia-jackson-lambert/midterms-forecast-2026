# v1 Against Other Forecasts as of 22 October

This note compares forecast v1, locked on 22 October 2026, with the other public forecasts and the two main prediction markets as they stood on the same day, and separately with their final pre-election numbers. Race calls are provisional (Associated Press, pulled 4 November 2026 10:41 UTC) and every score below will move as the remaining races are called. The tables beneath this introduction are generated by `python -m forecast.compare`; each source file in `results/comparisons/` records its URL and capture time.

Democrats won the Senate. On the 22 October numbers, v1's chance of control lay between DDHQ's and the markets' and below the Silver Bulletin topline. Race by race, the four probabilistic forecasts were close in the Senate (Brier 0.059 to 0.066 on the same 32 races) and all four had the favourite lose in the same three races. In the House, v1 scored worst of the probabilistic forecasts in competitive districts (Brier 0.210 against 0.176 for DDHQ and 0.100 for Polymarket on the same 38 races), and the ratings-based forecasts of Split Ticket (0.136) did better there too. In the governors' races v1 scored better than Cook's ratings and worse than both markets. The market prices were the most accurate input on every race-level comparison available, and they moved further towards the eventual result between 22 October and election eve.

## How to read the tables

- Probabilities are for the Democratic side as v1 defines it: the Democrat, or the independent where v1 places one (Nebraska, Idaho and South Dakota Senate). A race counts for that side when the winner is not a Republican.
- Market prices are reported as implied probabilities (the Democratic-side price divided by the sum of both sides' prices), not as models.
- Ratings are converted with a stated mapping (Toss Up 50%, Lean 65%, Likely 85%, Solid 97%, mirrored for Republicans) and scored in their own table. Rating scores depend heavily on that mapping and are not comparable with the probability scores.
- Each "common set" contains the called races that every forecast in that table covers; forecasts that cover under 90% of a chamber's called races (Kalshi's House markets, Sabato's listed House races) appear only in the head-to-head table, which compares each forecast with v1 on the races both cover. "Competitive" means the favourite had under 85% in at least one forecast in the comparison, so the competitive set differs between tables.
- Log loss uses natural logarithms with probabilities clipped to [0.001, 0.999]. "Upsets missed" counts called races the forecast's favourite lost, with a 50-50 forecast counted as half.
- Seat totals are not yet final: the outcome line in each section gives the seats among called races and the number still uncalled.

## Caveats

- **DDHQ's 22 October numbers.** The values scored for DDHQ are the 22 October points of its own daily time series, served on 4 November. They agree with every DDHQ figure Newsweek reported on 23 October (Senate 57% and 51 seats; Alaska 61%, Ohio 57%, Maine 56%, Michigan 68%, Texas 57%, Kansas and South Carolina Republican 65% and 70%). The Hill's page as archived on 22 October still showed the model of 14 October (Senate 56%), which is the figure recorded in issue #25. Scored on the same races, the 14 October numbers give a Senate Brier of 0.065 and a House Brier of 0.023, against 0.064 and 0.022 for the 22 October numbers; the conclusions do not change.
- **Silver Bulletin.** Race-level probabilities, House seat projections and governors are behind the paywall and were not accessed. The public part of each model page shows only polling averages, and the free posts of 19 to 25 October quote no 22 October model numbers. The Senate polling-average margins Newsweek reported are saved in `silverbulletin_poll_averages_2026-10-22.csv` but are not scored, since they are not forecasts.
- **Sabato's Crystal Ball** ratings were read by eye from archived images (Senate map of 14 October, House table of 20 October); colours that were hard to distinguish were checked against the legend's pixel values, and the totals agree with those printed on the map.
- **Markets.** Prices are the last hourly observation at or before the cut-off, so the effective time is up to an hour earlier (22:00 to 23:00 ET); a few thinly traded Kalshi markets use the bid-ask midpoint of the last candle. Where a race has both a party market and a candidate market, the party market is used.
- **Calls.** These are provisional. The races still uncalled include some of the closest, and v1's own score in issue #15 is kept separate from this comparison.
